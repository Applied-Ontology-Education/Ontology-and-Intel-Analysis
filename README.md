# Ontology Engineering and Intelligence Analysis

**Format:** Remote and asynchronous  
**Term:** Fall 2026  
**Duration:** 15 weeks

This course integrates foundational principles of ontology engineering with contemporary research in intelligence analysis. It explores ontological dimensions that inform intelligence methodologies, including questions concerning knowledge, truth, evidence, information, decision-making, bias, military intelligence, cybersecurity, threats, deception, and secrecy.

The course is designed around a shared sequence of readings, recorded lectures, ontology design-pattern exercises, and applied modeling activities. The same instructional materials support both undergraduate and graduate students, but the two tracks have different graded assessments.

## Start Here

1. Read this page from beginning to end.
2. Determine whether you are following the **Undergraduate Track** or **Graduate Track**.
3. Review the [Submission Guide](SUBMISSION.md).
4. Follow the weekly schedule below.
5. Use [GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/Applied-Ontology-Education/Ontology-and-Intel-Analysis/discussions) for discussion questions, presentation links, course questions, and project help.
6. Use the `readings/` and `presentations/` directories for the materials assigned in each week.

There are **no required synchronous class meetings**. Students should complete the readings, lectures, and assignments associated with each weekly module during the dates listed below.

## How the Course Progresses

The course moves through three broad stages:

1. **Foundations and methods:** ontology, intelligence analysis, top-down and bottom-up modeling, information artifacts, and design patterns.
2. **Applied intelligence domains:** military intelligence, cybersecurity, external and internal threats, and intelligence-relevant information structures.
3. **Advanced conceptual problems:** truth, falsehood, deception, secrecy, and the construction of an ontology project for an intelligence-analysis purpose.

The final project asks students to bring these ideas together in a small but carefully designed ontology addressing a problem, domain, or workflow relevant to intelligence analysis.

The Final Project is an ontology development project focused on an intelligence-analysis problem, domain, or workflow.

Students should favor a **small, carefully designed, well-documented ontology** over a large ontology assembled without adequate attention to modeling quality.

## Getting Help

If you have a question that may help other students, ask it in GitHub Discussions rather than sending a private message.

When asking for technical or modeling help, include:

- the week or assignment;
- what you are trying to accomplish;
- the relevant ontology fragment, diagram, or example;
- what you have already tried; and
- the specific point at which you are stuck.

Do not place passwords, API keys, classified information, controlled information, proprietary information, personal data, or other sensitive material in this public repository.
