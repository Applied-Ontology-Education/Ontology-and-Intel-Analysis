# Graduate Presentations

Graduate students complete two recorded asynchronous presentations during the semester.

The purpose of the presentations is to develop the ability to communicate and defend an ontology design pattern addressing an intelligence-analysis problem.

Students should select their presentation topic and presentation windows by the end of Week 2.

## Presentation Windows

Presentation windows are available during:

- Week 3
- Week 4
- Week 6
- Week 7
- Week 10
- Week 11
- Week 12
- Week 13
- Week 15

Students complete **two** presentations, not one presentation every presentation week.

## Presentation 1

**Recommended length:** 8–10 minutes.

Presentation 1 should introduce and defend an ontology design pattern addressing a clearly identified intelligence-analysis problem.

The presentation should explain:

1. **Problem.** What intelligence-analysis problem, domain, or workflow is being addressed?
2. **Intended use.** What question, task, or information need motivates the pattern?
3. **Competency questions.** What should the pattern allow a user or system to ask?
4. **Entities and relations.** What are the major entities and relations in the model?
5. **Reuse.** Which existing ontology terms, patterns, or standards are relevant?
6. **Modeling decisions.** What important decisions were made, and why?
7. **Limitations.** What remains unresolved?

A diagram is strongly encouraged.

## Presentation 2

**Recommended length:** 10–15 minutes.

Presentation 2 should normally present a substantially revised and improved version of Presentation 1.

It should address the elements above and additionally explain:

1. what changed since Presentation 1;
2. what feedback, evidence, or modeling problem motivated the changes;
3. what ontology content has now been implemented;
4. how the model has been evaluated or tested;
5. what remains uncertain or controversial; and
6. how the work could become the Final Project.

A different topic may be used for Presentation 2, but developing one pattern iteratively is strongly encouraged.

## Presentation Quality

Presentations are evaluated on:

- relevance to intelligence analysis;
- quality and coherence of the ontology design pattern;
- engagement with course concepts and readings;
- quality of modeling decisions and justification;
- clarity of explanation;
- effective use of diagrams, ontology fragments, or examples;
- responsiveness to feedback, especially for Presentation 2; and
- originality and insight.

## Submission

Presentations are submitted asynchronously.

Post a link to the recording in the designated **Graduate Presentations** GitHub Discussion.

See the [Submission Guide](../../SUBMISSION.md) and [Using GitHub Discussions](../../DISCUSSIONS.md).

No live presentation is required unless separately arranged.

## Public Posting

A presentation should not be publicly reposted outside the course environment without the presenter's permission.
