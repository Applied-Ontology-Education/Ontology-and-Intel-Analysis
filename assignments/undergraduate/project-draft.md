# Undergraduate Final Project Draft

**Recommended submission:** Week 9

The purpose of the Final Project Draft is to ensure that the proposed ontology project is:

- appropriately scoped;
- substantively connected to intelligence analysis;
- feasible for completion by the end of the course; and
- already supported by substantive preliminary modeling work.

Final projects must concern a problem, domain, or workflow relevant to intelligence analysis.

## Possible Project Areas

Appropriate topics include, but are not limited to:

- actors, organizations, events, capabilities, locations, threats, intentions, or relationships;
- concepts used in structured analytic techniques;
- evidence, hypotheses, assumptions, indicators, or competing explanations;
- intelligence requirements, collection requirements, or information needs;
- uncertainty, confidence, provenance, or source reliability;
- geopolitical, military, security, cyber, economic, or other intelligence-relevant domains;
- persons, organizations, locations, events, plans, resources, or relationships relevant to intelligence assessments;
- intelligence reporting or analytic products;
- semantic interoperability across intelligence datasets or systems;
- evaluation or improvement of an AI-generated ontology for an intelligence-analysis use case; or
- ontology content intended to ground, constrain, or evaluate an AI-supported intelligence workflow.

Use only public, fictional, synthetic, or otherwise authorized information.

## Draft Packet

### 1. Project Title and Intelligence-Analysis Topic

Identify the intelligence domain, analytic problem, or workflow the ontology will address.

### 2. Project Objective

Approximately 250 words explaining:

- what you intend to model;
- why the topic is relevant to intelligence analysis; and
- what analytic task or information need the ontology is intended to support.

### 3. Scope Statement

Explain what the ontology will represent and what will remain outside its scope.

### 4. Competency Questions or Intended Uses

Provide a preliminary set of questions, tasks, or use cases the ontology should support.

Frame these, where possible, in terms of intelligence-analysis needs.

### 5. Preliminary Ontology Artifact

Provide substantive evidence that ontology development has begun.

This may include:

- an initial OWL/RDF ontology file;
- a class hierarchy;
- a design-pattern diagram;
- definitions;
- relations;
- preliminary axioms; or
- another substantive modeling artifact.

### 6. Sources and Reused Resources

Provide a preliminary list of relevant:

- scholarly sources;
- intelligence-analysis resources;
- standards;
- datasets;
- existing ontologies; and
- other materials informing the project.

Clearly identify any ontology content you intend to reuse or adapt.

## Evaluation

The draft is evaluated on:

- viability and appropriate scope;
- relevance to intelligence analysis;
- engagement with course concepts and methods;
- quality of preliminary ontology work;
- clarity of the modeling approach;
- organization; and
- creativity.
